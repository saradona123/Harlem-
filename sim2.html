What is the "Anthropocene"?

The current geological age in which human activity is the dominant influence on climate, the environment, and life on Earth.

Format: 44 Personal Essays Scale: 1 to 5 Stars
Step 01 • No Traditional Plot

How the Book Is Structured

There is no fictional storyline or villain. Instead, it is a collection of non-fiction personal essays (adapted from John Green's podcast) where he reviews random parts of human existence on a 5-star scale.

Step 02 • The Formula

History + Memoir + Critique

Every chapter starts with fun facts or history about an everyday object (like Diet Dr Pepper or scratch 'n' sniff stickers) and smoothly pivots into deep personal memories about anxiety, love, and being human.

Step 03 • The Big Twist

Reviewing the Reviewer

By the end of the book, you realize John isn't really rating geese or comets—he is rating his own life and learning how to fall in love with a flawed, messy world.

5 Golden Quotes & Takeaways

  • 1. Central Paradox: Humans are both the biggest threat to Earth and the only species capable of appreciating its beauty.
  • 2. Why 5-Star Ratings? Star ratings are absurd when applied to sunsets or human grief—and that absurdity shows the limits of trying to measure life objectively.
  • 3. Famous Quote: "We are all just walking each other home" / "Pay attention to what you pay attention to."
  • 4. Tone & Style: Vulnerable, self-deprecating, witty, deeply empathetic, and conversational.
  • 5. Best Essay to Mention: "Capacity for Wonder" or "Auld Lang Syne"—both tie the entire book together!
